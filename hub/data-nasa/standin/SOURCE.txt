The hub's stand-in values (core/standin.ts), demo mode only

What this is
  In demo mode the hub fills every room's readings that no real or replayed source gives.
  The chip measures pressure, temperature, humidity, noise, light and vibration in its one
  room (the main cabin). NASA's ISS record (data-nasa/osdr-rr1) is replayed as every room's
  CO2, temperature and humidity. The stand-in makes up the rest: pressure, oxygen, noise,
  light, vibration, air quality and radiation. Every stand-in row is kind "simulated",
  source "sim:hub". Production mode makes none of it. The stand-in never makes CO2 on a
  mission: the only CO2 is NASA's record, and CO2 is the only signal with a NASA limit.

Three classes of number
  (a) cited: from a source already in this project, quoted with where it is.
  (b) convention: a plain physical or engineering convention.
  (c) made up for plausibility with no source.

Radiation (the signal where the place matters most)
  Two different quantities, never converted into each other. Each place uses the unit of
  the source that applies, and every row carries it.

  Earth orbit (the ISS)                                   uGy/day, absorbed dose      (a)
    Mir 19, 23 Jun to 20 Nov 1995, passive dosimeters, US dose rate in uGy/day:
    "commander's cabin 352.5; end of core module / exercise area 414.0; close to R-16 261.0;
    flight engineer's cabin 292.8; central control panel 279.0; transfer module window 336.0"
    (NASA Life Sciences Portal, dataset J0001100; Badhwar GD et al., "Radiation Environment
    on the Mir Orbital Station During Solar Minimum", Adv. Space Research;
    ~/Desktop/NASA/data-found/DATA-FOUND.md section 10). Mir is not the ISS; both fly inside
    Earth's magnetic field: "missions to the space station are entirely within the
    magnetosphere, missions to the Moon and Mars will extend beyond this protective bubble"
    (NASA, Risk of Radiation-Induced Cancers; passages.json, risk-radiation-induced-cancers-3).
    Each room takes the Mir location of like use: quarters the commander's and the flight
    engineer's cabins in turn, the exercise room the exercise area, the command room the
    control panel, a window room the transfer module window; every other room takes the six
    rates in turn, by room order.

  Mars orbit, transit, Moon orbit (the ship)              mSv/day, dose equivalent    (a)
    1.8: "an average GCR dose equivalent rate of 1.8 millisieverts per day" in transit to
    Mars (Curiosity RAD; Zeitlin et al., Science 2013, 340:1080; DATA-FOUND.md section 11).
    It was measured in cruise, not in Mars orbit; it is the project's only figure for space
    beyond Earth's magnetic field.

  Mars surface (the Mars base)                            mSv/day, dose equivalent    (a)
    0.67: "0.67 millisieverts per day from August 2012 to June 2013 on the Martian surface"
    (Curiosity RAD; Hassler et al., Science 2014; DATA-FOUND.md section 11).

  Moon surface (the Moon base)                            mSv/day                     (c)
    About 1. No cited figure in this project. Made up for plausibility with no source: a
    round number between the two RAD figures, chosen so it does not pretend to precision.

  Earth's surface                                         none
    No place in this project stands there and no figure exists here: no radiation row.

  Rooms inside one place (mSv/day places)                 pattern only                (a)
    The place's figure times (that room's Mir rate / the mean of the six Mir rates, 322.55),
    using the same room-to-location rule as above. Mir's spread is reused as a pattern only;
    no unit is converted. Rooms run from 0.81 to 1.28 times the place's figure.

  Wander                                                                              (c)
    The sources give an average, not its spread. The shown value moves about one display
    step (1 uGy/day, or 0.01 mSv/day) every ten minutes and stays within a step or two of
    the centre. Made up for plausibility with no source.

Pressure                         1013.25 hPa, every place                             (b)
  Sea-level standard atmosphere, the same 760 mmHg the CO2 replay is converted at
  (osdr-rr1/SOURCE.txt). NASA says deep-space craft will run at "a constant partial cabin
  depressurization" (Risk of Hypoxia; passages.json, risk-hypoxia-1) but no figure for it is
  in this project, so it is not applied. Wander about 0.05 hPa a second (c).

Oxygen                           159.2 mmHg, every place                              (b)
  20.95 % of dry air at 760 mmHg. Wander as before (c).

Gravity                                                                               -
  Changes nothing here. The bases keep the same sea-level cabin as the spacecraft, and no
  room reading is moved by gravity.

Light (raw 0-4095, like the chip's light sensor)
  Rhythm (a): "light-emitting diodes, or LEDs, simulate Earth's light-dark cycle to help
  astronauts sleep better" (NASA, Risk from Inadequate Sleep and Irregular Schedules;
  passages.json, risk-sleep-2). The dark hours are the simulated crew's sleep, 22:00 to
  06:00 (engine/crew.ts), on the hub's 24-hour clock in every place. A Martian day is
  longer than 24 hours; the demo does not keep a sol clock.
  Levels (c), made up for plausibility with no source: by day 2400 in work rooms (lab,
  command, medical, greenhouse), 2000 in the galley and exercise room, 1400 in quarters,
  1200 elsewhere; at night 30 in quarters, 500 elsewhere.
  A window room in Earth orbit (the Cupola): sunlight comes and goes every 90 minutes,
  from "Astronauts witness 16 sunrises and sunsets per day aboard the space station"
  (passages.json, risk-sleep-3) (a); its brightness, up to 1200 more, is (c).

Noise (raw 0-4095, like the chip's microphone)                                        (c)
  Made up for plausibility with no source: 240 at rest (the stand-in's old value); a lab
  +100 day and night (equipment); command and medical +40; +40 for each person present;
  the galley +90 for each person eating there; quarters at night 150; other rooms 40 lower
  at night. The exercise room +1000, and +100 for each person exercising, while someone is
  exercising there: someone counts as exercising during their 1.5-hour block in the
  simulated crew's day, which keeps NASA's "1.5 hours per day" (passages.json,
  risk-reduced-fitness-2) (a); a crewmate who only works there does not.

Vibration (g)                                                                         (c)
  Made up for plausibility with no source: 0.002 on a spacecraft (pumps, fans, thrusters),
  0.001 on a surface base, +0.02 while someone is exercising in the exercise room.

Air quality (index)              1.4, as before                                       (c)
  Shown as a whole index, so it reads 1.

Temperature and humidity         only when NASA's replay is silent                    (c)
  23 C (0.3 C lower at night) and 45 %, as before. In demo mode NASA's replay gives both in
  every room, so these are rarely seen.

Without a mission
  An old profile file, or the recorded story ("npm run scenario"), runs the stand-in as it
  always was: one ordinary cabin per signal, CO2 included (2.4 mmHg, c), and the six Mir
  rates in uGy/day handed to the rooms in order.

How it moves
  Each value drifts randomly toward its target, a little each second (the random function
  is passed in, so tests are repeatable), never below 0, and the shown value changes only by
  a whole display step, as a sensor's reading does. Nothing it makes is near a NASA limit:
  the only limit is CO2's, and it makes no CO2 on a mission.
