## The flow and why

**The phone is personal.** The hub shows the station. The phone belongs to one crewmate and shows that person's body and what concerns them. So the home tab is *you*, not the station, and anything about other people is only what the hub shares on its own screens.

**Me.** Your body sits in the middle and you explore it. Five regions (head, heart, lungs and air, body and activity, blood), each tied to its readings. A region gets a mark when one of its readings is outside your usual or part of an alert. Under it, one line sums up today ("Everything within your usual", "2 readings outside your usual", or the alert), then the readings as a list. The figure is how you find things; the list is where you read them. Tapping a region filters the list. Tapping a reading opens its detail: your usual (median and spread), its history, its label and source in words, and, when the hub's evidence card covers it, what it points to. *Code:* `Screens/Me/` (`MeView`, `AvatarView`, `ReadingRow`, `ReadingDetailView`), data in `Model/Readings.swift`.

**Check-in.** This is where you enter things: the daily questions (headache yes/no; mood, stress and tiredness 0 to 100; an optional note) and the blood test entries. Only what the hub accepts. Answers come back labelled Reported. Offline, a check-in is kept on the phone and sent with the time you answered it. *Code:* `Screens/CheckIn/`.

**Ask Astra.** A Messages-style chat titled "Astra" (see "Ask Astra as a chat" below). Your questions on the right in blue, Astra's answers on the left as the hub sent them, the composer at the bottom. The model runs on the hub. Out of reach, the composer is off with the reason and the saved thread stays readable. *Code:* `Screens/Ask/`, `Model/AskThread.swift`.

**Station.** The companion's small window on the hub: the room you are in and its air (CO2 against NASA's 2.8 and 3), the crew as names, rooms and shared status, the place and mission day, and Earth (the delay, Contact Earth, messages with countdowns, the simulated reply marked Simulated). *Code:* `Screens/Station/`.

**Alerts.** Full screen over any tab, and only alerts you are told about. Level 2 about you: "Are you OK?", the countdown, two big answers. Level 3 about anyone: who, where, the number against the limit with its label, the steps card word for word (or "No steps card is approved for this yet"), Earth's countdown, "I've seen it". Once seen it can be closed and stays on Me as a banner until it ends. Level 1 is never full screen. A tapped notification opens the alert. *Code:* `Screens/Alerts/`.

**First run and Settings.** Find the hub, choose who you are, Health, notifications. Settings keeps what it had and adds language (iOS's per-app language: it follows the device unless changed in the Settings app) and "Open the hub's page", the one place the web view is still used. *Code:* `Screens/Onboarding/`, `SettingsView.swift`.

## Where the data comes from

- `Model/HubWorld.swift`: everything the socket has sent, applied the way `hub/ui/src/data.ts` applies it, on the hub's clock. Saved to disk, so Me opens offline with "Not connected · showing data from 14:05".
- `Model/Timeline.swift` and `Model/Baseline.swift`: the hub's rules ported from TypeScript. The current value: among fresh readings, measured beats estimated beats reported beats replayed beats simulated. Your usual: median and scaled MAD of your own history, the same as the hub's person view.
- `Model/HubAPI.swift`: every HTTP route, run and decoded off the main actor.
- `Preview/SampleData.swift`: the one file of sample data. Every screen has a `#Preview`. Previews only; the running app shows only what the hub sent.

## Deliberately unstyled

- **The body figure** is the hub's 3D crew avatar (`hub/ui/src/avatar/`), built into `Resources/avatar.html` by `hub/ui/src/avatar/phone.ts` and shown by `AvatarView`. Head, heart (heart view), lungs (breathing view) and body (arms, legs, pelvis) light up on the figure; blood shows the heart view's vessels without the heart. Marked regions are outlined in orange. The region row under it stays: it is the VoiceOver and Switch Control path.
- **The range bar** (`Components/RangeBar.swift`) is a hairline, a band and a dot. It has three uses: your usual, NASA's marks, a daily target.
- **The chart** is a default Swift Charts line with a band.
- **Lists and forms** are stock `List` and `Form` in the dark scheme. No custom colours, materials, spacing or type scale.
- **Alerts** are a black screen with big bordered buttons. No motion at all, so nothing needs a Reduce Motion fallback yet. If you add some, gate it.
- **Tab icons** are placeholder SF Symbols.

## Blood analyser: reading its screen (14–15 Nov)

- **What it is:** a camera bridge, not a connection. Live camera (or a chosen photo) → prepared picture → Vision on the phone → `Model/BloodScan.swift` finds the hub's seven values by the analyser's labels → `Model/BloodScanTally.swift` keeps only numbers read the same way several times → a review screen where each value is confirmed or typed → `POST /api/blood` with `from_photo` (hub stores those as `istat:photo`, still `measured`). Code: `Screens/CheckIn/BloodScanView.swift`, `Screens/CheckIn/BloodScanCamera.swift`, `Model/BloodScanReader.swift`, `Model/BloodScan.swift`, `Model/BloodScanTally.swift`.
- **Why many frames, not one still, and not `DataScannerViewController`:** one still of a photographed screen is read right only about a third of the time with the first reader (below). Reading many frames and keeping only what agrees is what makes it reliable. The system live scanner gives no control over the agreement rule, no picture to check against and does not run the macOS test; frames + `VNRecognizeTextRequest` do.
- **Where values were lost (first reader, simulated phone photos of a monitor):** mostly not the parser but the OCR: on a blurred, moiré-covered frame Vision dropped whole rows or the number of a row (label read, number not), and read a unit as noise ("mno1/"), which made the old parser drop the value. Single frames also read wrong numbers now and then ("4.5" as "5", "141" as "143", "468" as "458").
- **What changed:** 4K frames inside a framing guide; the picture squared up to the screen and given local contrast (measured: +10 to +17 points of values per frame; softening, global contrast, upscaling to a text height, sharpening and inverting did not help or hurt, so they are off); a number must have exactly the decimals the analyser shows (K "5" is refused); Vision's second and third guesses must not read another number; a misread unit like "*PCV" is no longer a flag; an unread unit is offered (marked) only where no other unit could pass; and the tally: 3 agreeing frames, never a different number.
- **Not done, on purpose:** matching a misread label by its row position. Measured, labels were rarely the only thing missing, and the frames fill in what one frame misses; a position-based guess was not needed to reach the target.
- **Layouts read** (Abbott i-STAT 1 System Manual, "Interpretation of displayed results" and "Set units"; i-STAT Alinity Quick Reference Guide, results pages): i-STAT 1 rows `Na mmol/L … 141`, `Glu mg/dL`, `Hct %PCV`, `pH` with no unit, arrows after a number past an action range; Alinity tiles `Na, mmol/L` with the number under it. Both: `***`, `<>`, `<`/`>`.
- **Plausibility limits** are the analyser's reportable ranges (Na 100–180, K 2.0–9.0, iCa 0.25–2.50 mmol/L, Glu 20–700 mg/dL, pH 6.50–8.20, Hct 10–75 %PCV, Hb 3.4–25.5 g/dL). Decimals: exactly Na 0, K 1, Glu 0, Hct 0, pH 3 (every Abbott example agrees); iCa up to 2, Hb up to 1.
- **Test harness (macOS, no app build), in `/private/tmp/bloodscan-test/`:** `main.swift` (93 parser and tally checks) compiles with `Model/BloodScan.swift` and `Model/BloodScanTally.swift`; `sim/` compiles the same files plus `Model/BloodScanReader.swift` with a simulator of "phone pointed at a monitor": the picture in a window on a screen with RGB subpixels and black gaps, re-photographed at 4K with slant, turn, moiré, glare, defocus, noise and JPEG, the screen filling 60–95 % of the frame; sequences add hand movement and autofocus hunting (one frame in five badly out of focus).
- **Results, 15 Nov (120 sequences of 30 frames, 24 per picture):** single still with the first reader: 32 % of values, 26 % of screens complete, 1 wrong value filled. Single still, prepared: 49 %, 37 %, 0 wrong. Live: 87 % of values, 84 % of screens complete, 0 wrong. By picture, live: Abbott's i-STAT 1 EC8+ 24/24 screens complete, EG7+ 24/24, Alinity CHEM8+ 23/24 (one iCa unit unread), Alinity control 24/24: **95 of 96 (99 %) on the clean pictures**. The photo-like test copy (`istat1-ec8-photo.jpg`, already blurred, glared and small, then shown on a monitor and filmed) reaches only 6/24: its numbers are light grey and soft before the camera even sees them. With the whole sequence out of focus (40 sequences): 73 % of screens complete, 0 wrong. Across everything: 0 wrong values filled by the new paths.
- **Only a build and a real analyser can tell:** the camera on a phone (focus, exposure, frame rate of Vision), a real LCD's glare and backlight, the real label spellings (for example `Hb` or `Hb*`), and the screens' layout on a phone.
- **Keyboard (also for typed results):** previous / next / Done over the keyboard on every Check-in field and the review screen, drag down to dismiss, a tap on a field's name or caption dismisses, the focused field scrolls into view. Long names wrap; the unit is on the line under the name.

## Design references to open on your phone

- **Apple Watch / Health "Vitals"** (the overnight view). Values plotted against *your* typical range, "within/outside typical" in plain words, one line on top. *Borrow:* the range-against-yourself bar, and the calm when nothing is wrong. *Don't copy:* the nightly-only framing. Our readings arrive all day.
- **Health app, Summary and a body-measurement detail page** (e.g. Resting Heart Rate). Pinned list of latest values, then a detail page with a chart, ranges and "About". *Borrow:* the detail's order (value, chart, context) and the D/W/M range switcher if you want longer history. *Don't copy:* the colourful category tints. Our status colours mean something is wrong.
- **Fitness rings.** A daily target filled through the day. *Borrow:* a ring or bar for exercise against NASA's 1.5 hours. *Don't copy:* streaks, awards and goals the user sets. The target is NASA's, not a habit game.
- **Emergency SOS countdown and Medical ID.** Huge countdown, two unmistakable actions, information readable by someone else. *Borrow:* the type scale and the slide-or-hold patterns if you want "I need help" to be harder to hit by accident. Medical ID's plain layout fits the level 3 screen another crewmate reads. *Don't copy:* the auto-call behaviour. The hub decides who is told.
- **Check In in Messages.** "Are you OK?", a timer, and what happens if you don't answer. *Borrow:* saying up front what happens when the time runs out ("the crew is told"). *Don't copy:* the casual tone.
- **Oura's or WHOOP's daily readiness screen.** One summary up top, details below. *Borrow:* one line over the details. *Don't copy:* a single invented score. Astrum shows which readings are outside your usual and never folds them into one number.
- **Health app's Medications log** (logging a dose). *Borrow:* for Check-in, fast tap-to-answer rows and the history underneath. *Don't copy:* reminders and schedules nobody asked for.

## Open questions (yours)

1. **Figure or list first?** Does the body earn its space every day, or should it shrink once a region is picked? The plain region row under it is there for VoiceOver and might be all some people want.
2. **What sits in the middle of Me when something is wrong?** Today the summary line and the region mark. Should the most unusual reading become a hero card, as the hub's person view does?
3. **Blood as a region.** It holds lab results, not live values. Keep it on the figure, or move it to Check-in only?
4. **Check-in cadence.** Once a day, or whenever? Should Me nudge when today's check-in is missing (careful: the hub pushes nothing like that today)?
5. **Sliders' meaning.** Mood, stress and tiredness are 0 to 100 with no anchors, because the hub defines none. Do you want words at the ends ("none", "worst")? That is a medical-lead question as much as a design one.
6. **Level 3 on someone else's phone.** Full screen until seen, then a banner. Right, or should it stay full screen until it ends, as on the hub?
7. **Station's weight.** Is it a tab, or a sheet from Me? It is the least personal screen.
8. **Language.** It uses iOS's per-app language (Settings app). Do you want an in-app switch for the demo instead?
9. **Freshness.** One rule (`Model/Freshness.swift`, the hub's `baseline.ts`): vital signs (heart rate, HRV, breathing rate, blood oxygen, blood pressure, body temperature) are current for one hour only, however rarely they arrive; daily values (sleep, steps, exercise, check-ins) for 36 hours, named with their time ("Sleep last night", "Steps today"); anything else one hour. Older is a gap: the row and detail say "No current reading" with the last time, no range bar, no comparison with the usual, no region mark, not counted in the summary line (an alert the hub raised still marks it). The hub's own rule for picking the current source is 30 seconds, and an Apple Watch heart rate arrives every few minutes. Do those windows feel right on screen?

## Ask Astra as a chat (14 Nov, tracker 519)

- **Look:** a 1:1 copy of the owner's Aimbot chat (`Aimbot/Views/ChatBubbles.swift`, `ChatTimes.swift`, and `MessageView`, `TypingIndicator` and the composer in `IntelligenceView.swift`), ported to iOS in `Screens/Ask/ChatBubbles.swift`: the same bubble outline with the tail on a run's last bubble (radius 17, continuous), Messages' grey (white 0.23) and blue (`systemBlue`), 12 × 7 padding, 4 between a run's bubbles and 12 between runs, a centred time ("Today 13:38") after a 15-minute gap, the three dots, the glass composer pill (an outline before iOS 26), and the example questions as suggestion pills over an empty thread. The one tab that follows Messages instead of the projection frames; the navigation bar, fonts and background stay the app's.
- **An answer** is an incoming run: Astra's or a fixed text's words as bubbles (one per paragraph), then attachments drawn like Messages' link previews: each hub fact with its label(s) and kind mark in the band under it, the steps card, NASA's quote with its source and site (a tap opens it). Under the run, where Messages says "Delivered": "Understood as: …" when the hub rewrote a follow-up, who wrote it, the route when it is not a plain answer, "Marked urgent", and "Answered at 14:05 in 2.4 s, from the readings then".
- **A question back** (`pendingQuestion`: her ask-first questions, a card's own yes/no questions, "Did it get better?", "Do you mean your sleep or your heart rate?") is the last incoming bubble, with its answers as suggestion chips under it (44 pt, VoiceOver "Reply: Yes"). A tap sends the label as your next message through the normal ask call; the hub reads it against the question and stores the check-in answer itself. Only the newest question's chips are live; older ones say "Replied: Yes" (or the replies offered). For a value, the composer's placeholder names the unit and the keyboard opens on numbers with letters one key away; typing anything always works. The first pass's direct `POST /api/checkin` from Yes / No is gone (it would have stored the answer twice); older saved threads still show what was answered then.
- **The hub's own sentences** (small talk, "Thank you, noted.", "which one?") are plain bubbles with only their time under them: no attachments, no "no facts" bubble, no "who wrote it" line.
- **Pending:** the typing dots, with the hub's real stage under them ("Sending the question…", "Waiting in line: position 2", "Astra is working: 4 s"). **Failed:** Messages' red mark beside the question, "Not Delivered" (out of reach) or "Not Answered" (the hub refused, in its words), and Try Again. One question at a time, as the hub answers one at a time.
- **Memory (15 Nov, tracker 533):** the hub keeps each crewmate's conversation for 30 minutes of quiet. With each question the phone sends `history`: this crewmate's answered exchanges still inside that half hour (walking back, each within 30 minutes of the next), at most 6, oldest first, each text cut to 600 UTF-16 units at a character boundary, and the oldest dropped until the body is under 9,000 bytes (the hub reads at most 10,000). The hub uses it only for the topic after a restart, and checks it for a crisis phrase. The top of the thread says once: "Astra remembers what this conversation is about for 30 minutes, so you can ask follow-ups like 'and yesterday?'. Each answer is still checked on its own."
- **Keyboard:** Send and the quick-reply chips leave the keyboard up. The app-wide tap-outside recogniser (`Components/KeyboardDismiss.swift`) skips any control marked `.keepsKeyboard()`, which reports its frame in the window.
- **Steps card footer** (Ask and alert screens): "This card says to message Earth.", "Approved by the medical lead." (the hub's `approvedBy`), and only when her `unverified` list is not empty "Parts not checked against a source: …" (behind a disclosure past two parts).
- **History:** `AskThread` keeps the last 50 exchanges per crewmate in `ask-<person>.json` (`LocalStore`), answers exactly as the hub sent them (`AskResult` is now `Codable`). The new fields are optional, so threads saved before them load unchanged. A question still on its way when the app closed comes back as failed, with Try Again. Clear (toolbar, with confirmation) deletes the file's contents.
- **Accessibility:** the thread reads top to bottom; your question is spoken "You asked, 14:05: …"; an answer is a container that announces when, who wrote it and the labels in words; each fact speaks its label; finished answers and failures are announced at low priority; the field is labelled; bubbles and attachments grow with Dynamic Type (labels stack at accessibility sizes, the far margin shrinks); Reduce Motion: no send motion, still dots, no animated scroll.
- **Not ported from Aimbot:** avatars and speaker names (a 1:1 chat with no face), Tapbacks, Reply and the other message actions (only Copy), the swipe-left to see each message's time (the centred times and the line under each answer carry it), voice and attachments from the composer, Return-to-send (iOS: the send button).
- **Only a build can tell:** the tail's look at real sizes, the glass pill on iOS 26 against the thread, the keyboard riding and interactive dismiss, the scroll staying at the bottom as answers arrive, VoiceOver reading the answer container's summary, the chips wrapping at large sizes, the keyboard staying up on Send and on a chip (the frame-based exemption), and the keyboard type switching for a value question while the field is focused.
- **Freshness check (no build):** from `phone/`, `swiftc -swift-version 5 Astrum/Model/Freshness.swift Astrum/Model/Baseline.swift Astrum/Model/HubTypes.swift Checks/Freshness/main.swift -o /tmp/freshness-check && /tmp/freshness-check` (vital signs one hour, daily values 36 hours, a 3-hour-old low blood oxygen not judged).

## Names and alert sentences (15 Nov)

- **One naming rule:** `Signals.name` is the only way a signal key becomes words (screens, notifications, widgets, chat facts, VoiceOver). Names are the hub's own (`strings.ts` `signal.*`, her `blood.json` names, her follow-up and symptom questions); blood keys are `blood.<id>` with `_` for `-`. A key nobody named yet becomes plain words (namespace dropped, `._-` to spaces, sentence case) and is logged in debug builds, never shown raw. Chat facts pass the hub's signal word through `Signals.inSentence`, so a raw `blood.glucose` from the hub's unusual-signal sentence reads "glucose".
- **Alert sentences** (`Model/AlertText.swift`), the same on the lock screen, alert screens, Me's banners and summary: a room over a limit keeps the hub's "Leo is in the Lab, where CO2 is over NASA's limit"; a person's own reading is "<Name>: <state>" ("Steps today: unusual for you", "Glucose: 250 mg/dL, past the medical lead's limit of 180"); her other findings say what happened ("Leo needs someone with them.", "You said it did not get better."). Notifications are now localized (EN + PT) and name rooms by the hub's room names.
- **Check (no build):** from `phone/`, `swiftc -swift-version 5 Astrum/Model/Signals.swift Astrum/Model/Freshness.swift Astrum/Model/HubTypes.swift Astrum/HubMessages.swift Checks/SignalNames/main.swift -o /tmp/signal-names && /tmp/signal-names ../hub`: reads the hub's string table, blood, follow-up and cards files and its source rows, and fails on any key without a written name.


## Motion (15 Nov)

Every animation reads from `Components/Motion.swift`; tune the feel there by changing a number. Reduce Motion: nothing moves or scales (changes cross-fade or just happen), haptics stay.

- **`Motion.select`** `.snappy(duration: 0.28)`: the white fill sliding between Me's region chips (`matchedGeometryEffect`), from a chip or the figure.
- **`Motion.content`** `.smooth(duration: 0.35)`: Me's rows changing with the region (List insert/remove, keyed on the region only, never the 10 s refresh), banners and the connection notice arriving or leaving, numbers rolling (`numericMotion`: widget big numbers, reading values, every `BigNumber` on Station), the reading rows' range-bar dot and the widget tracks gliding to a new value.
- **`Motion.appear`** `.easeOut(duration: 0.3)`: the check-in's confirmation line fading in.
- **`Motion.urgent`** `.easeOut(duration: 0.22)`: one alert screen cross-fading into the next inside the same full-screen cover ("Are you OK?" becoming dangerous, one danger giving way to another), instead of the cover sliding down and up again.
- **`Motion.reveal`** `.easeOut(duration: 0.6)`: the history chart drawing in from the left, once, when the detail opens (a fade under Reduce Motion).
- **`Motion.press`** `.spring(duration: 0.25, bounce: 0)`: `.pressable` (Me's widget cards) and `.projection` buttons scale to 0.98 while pressed and settle back; the dim stays under Reduce Motion.
- **`Motion.calm`** `.easeInOut(duration: 0.2)`: what animations become under Reduce Motion (`Motion.reduced`); pure movement becomes none (`Motion.movement`).
- **Transition** `.astrumRise`: in, fade + 8 pt rise; out, fade (opacity only under Reduce Motion). Ready for non-List views; List rows use the system's row animation.
- **Navigation:** a Me widget card zooms into its detail (`matchedTransitionSource` + `.zoom`); a reading row pushes plainly. `ReadingRouter.zoomSource` remembers which, so the way back matches the way in.
- **Haptics:** selection on a region change; success when a check-in is sent and when the hub confirms "I am OK". No haptic of our own when "Are you OK?" or danger appears: their notification is presented with sound even in the app (`AstrumApp` `willPresent`), so it would buzz twice.
- **Left as the system's:** the full-screen cover's own entrance (slides up), sheets, tab changes, scrolling, the loading cover's fade, Ask Astra.
